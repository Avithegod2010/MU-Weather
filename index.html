Explore MU Weather

Open a section to see what you can do in the Android app.

Forecasts and chartsHourly details · 16-day outlook
  • Right now. See temperature, wind, humidity and pressure. When it feels much hotter or colder than the reading, the app explains why.
  • Hour by hour. Tap an hour for rain chance and amount, feels-like temperature, gusts, UV, visibility and more.
  • Plan up to 16 days ahead. Open a day for temperature and rain curves, sunrise and sunset, and expected UV and wind.
  • Explore the trends. Scrub interactive charts across 24 hours, seven days, or the past 30 days. Highlights call out changes like frost, gusts and rain arriving soon.
Radar and mapsRain playback · Weather layers
  • Replay rain. Play or scrub the last two hours of RainViewer frames. Pinch to zoom, pan or recenter. The app renders its own tiles without a Google Maps SDK, billing account or key.
  • See what’s moving in. Minute-level rain outlooks describe when bands are arriving, easing or passing.
  • Switch layers. Explore precipitation, temperature, wind and cloud. Active US National Weather Service warnings can appear as outlines on the radar.
Alerts and warnings17 alert rules · Official warnings
  • Pick your alerts. Opt in to rules for rain, storms, fog, black ice, heat, frost, air quality and more, including for saved cities.
  • Check what was triggered. Alert history shows up to 20 outcomes with the reason, city and local time. Android can defer background checks.
  • See official warnings. Supported areas in Europe, the US and Canada receive warnings from MeteoAlarm, the US National Weather Service and Environment and Climate Change Canada. The app shows the source and expiry.
  • Listen to the forecast. Read the forecast aloud, use the daily digest’s spoken forecast action, or set an alarm before sunrise with a choice of four tones.
Air quality and pollenAQI forecast · Six pollen types
  • Check the air. Switch between US and European AQI scales, see the hourly curve and five-day peak outlook, and view pollutant breakdowns such as PM2.5 and ozone.
  • Compare pollen levels. See six pollen types and which one is highest. Pollen forecasts are available in Europe through CAMS.
Sun, moon and skyGolden hour · Aurora · Storm tools
  • Time the light. Check sunrise, sunset, golden hour, moon phase and illumination.
  • Look for auroras. See the Kp index, visibility outlook and solar wind at supported latitudes.
  • Explore storms. See when forecast instability peaks and use the storm distance timer to time the gap between lightning and thunder.
Trips and outdoor plansTrip forecast · Calendar events
  • Plan a trip. Pick a saved city and travel dates for a day-by-day outlook and a summary of how many days look wet.
  • Check your calendar. See the forecast for upcoming events and a rain-risk note when relevant.
  • Pick a time outdoors. Compare two-hour windows scored on rain, wind, UV and feels-like temperature, along with a weekend comparison.
  • Dress for the day. Rate how the day felt in the on-device journal. It tracks your rating streak, learns at what temperatures you tend to feel hot or cold, and uses that to tailor clothing suggestions.
Themes and widgets10 themes · 6 Android widgets
  • Make the app yours. Choose from 10 colour themes, including AMOLED black and wallpaper-based Material You on Android 12 and newer. App surfaces and charts follow the selected palette.
  • Adjust the layout. Choose a material or glass style, compact or comfortable spacing, and your icon style. Pick which home tiles appear and how they animate.
  • See the weather on your home screen. Choose from 2×2 current conditions, a sunrise or sunset countdown, a 4×2 hourly widget, a 4×3 dashboard, a configurable saved-city widget and a radar snapshot. Widget colours follow the current sky and time of day.
  • Use your language. Choose from 16 languages, including English, Hindi, Bengali and Spanish. See all 16 languages. The app supports TalkBack and reduced motion.
History and climate30-day history · A year of daily highs
  • Look back. Compare recent weather with the app’s saved forecasts and see wet or dry streaks.
  • Explore the yearly heatmap. See a 365-day grid of daily highs plus the hottest, coldest and wettest days.
  • Compare with local averages. Check monthly climate normals and look up what happened on today’s date over the past decade.
Forecast accuracyPast forecast scores · Model comparison
  • See how the app did. The app checks logged forecasts against later archive observations and reports how far off its predictions were.
  • Read uncertainty. The P10–P90 band shows the range between the 10th and 90th percentiles of model runs for temperature and wind. Rain charts can show how many model runs predict rain when enough runs support the comparison.
  • Compare models and export data. Compare supported models by city and metric when enough shared dates are available, including ECMWF, GFS, ICON, Météo-France and JMA. Export the app’s forecast log as CSV or JSON.

How it works

MU Weather fetches data directly from public services. There’s no MU Weather server in between.

  1. Choose a locationSearch for a city or allow location access. Weather requests typically use a coordinate rounded to about 1 km and, where needed, your display language.
  2. Fetch public weatherYour phone makes HTTPS requests directly to the relevant providers. Standard forecasts, maps and warnings require no account or API key.
  3. Save locallyRecent data is cached in AsyncStorage, with expiration times and size limits. Saved forecasts are labelled when stale.
  4. See or hear itRead the forecast on Material You screens, check a home-screen widget, or listen to the daily digest.

Privacy and offline use

The app has no account, ads, analytics, crash reporting or MU Weather servers. Data for weather requests goes directly to public providers. Settings, journal ratings, saved cities, alert history and caches live on your device.

You can search for a city without granting location permission. Calendar access is used when you choose to see forecasts next to events. Notifications are used for the alerts, digest and sunrise alarm you enable.

Frequently asked questions

Is MU Weather really free?

Yes. MU Weather is open source under the MIT license. The Android app has no ads, accounts or paid tier. Its regular weather features use public data sources.

Does it need an internet connection?

Previously fetched forecasts and air-quality data can still be read offline. An internet connection is needed for fresh data, including new radar frames and warnings. The app labels stale data.

What is the optional AI digest?

The standard daily digest can read the forecast aloud without an AI key. The source supports an optional rewrite by Anthropic or OpenAI when an AI key is configured at build time. In that mode the provider receives the digest text, city name and language code. The app falls back to the standard digest when the AI service is unavailable.

Is there an iOS version?

The published downloads are for Android. The app is built with React Native and uses Android features such as Material You, home-screen widgets and launcher shortcuts.

How accurate is the forecast?

The app compares logged predictions with later archive observations and lets you inspect accuracy by metric and model. Its leaderboard includes models such as ECMWF, GFS, ICON, Météo-France and JMA where there are enough observations. Rain calibration uses local observations and feedback when enough samples are available.

How can I verify an APK?

Compare its SHA-256 checksum with the value in the download section. The Android release was built with Expo EAS and is linked to a public GitHub tag so you can inspect the corresponding source.

What data does MU Weather collect?

The Android app has no accounts, analytics, ads, crash reporting services or servers of its own. Public weather providers receive the information needed for each request. Saved cities, journal ratings, settings, caches and alert history are stored on your device. See the full privacy policy.
